Biyahe: town by the bay

Draft plan for Ian · 2026-10-29 · nothing built yet

The jeepney leaves the island for where jeepneys actually live: a small Filipino town on a bay. A plaza, a jeepney terminal at the pier, a loop of streets, and the six projects as real places along the route. Everything stays built in code (no model files), in the same flat signboard palette, with the shaders and sound we already have.

1. The town

  • Size: about 80 × 80 m of town (the island is 54 m across), a 3 × 3 grid of blocks with 8 m streets.
  • The plaza sits in the middle block: fountain, four shade trees, benches, the town's heart.
  • The jeepney route (red dashes) loops round the plaza and out along the bay boulevard past the terminal. Driving stays free: you can go anywhere on the streets, the route is just what the autopilot and the road paint follow.
  • The bay runs along the top. The sea shader, foam, glints, cloud shadows and the pier all carry over.
  • Tall things go north (top of the map, the far side from the camera), as now: Lumenfold's roof lamp (3) and Bagyo's mast (4) stand there. The south row stays low.
  • The other blocks are ordinary town: shophouses, a sari-sari store on a corner, the basketball court, electric poles with their tangle of wires.

2. The six stops, reimagined

Rough front sketches: proportions and ideas, not final art. Each stop gets one thing that moves, so it reads as alive from a distance.

Stop 1 · FashionHub

Ukay-ukay boutique

  • Two-storey shophouse: shop below, capiz-shell windows above (bahay na bato style).
  • Display window with three mannequins, striped awning, roll-up shutter half open.
  • Moves: clothes on the sidewalk rack swing in the wind (the sway shader).
  • South row, low (≈7 m). Footprint ≈ 8 × 6 m.
Stop 2 · Sprig & Stone

Corner plant shop

  • Corner shophouse taken over by plants: a balcony of hanging pots, tiers of pots spilling onto the sidewalk.
  • Shade-net awning over the shopfront, a bamboo trellis with vines up the side wall.
  • Moves: hanging pots and vines sway; a few leaves drift down.
  • West block, facing the plaza. ≈ 7 m tall.
Stop 3 · Lumenfold

Lamp shop with a giant roof lamp

  • Narrow three-storey building, every window glowing warm and full of lamps.
  • A giant folding desk lamp on the roof, the Lumenfold lamp itself, as the landmark you see from across town.
  • Moves: the lamp slowly turns and nods, sweeping a soft beam over the street (the lighthouse beam shader, reused).
  • North row (tall, far side). ≈ 11 m with the lamp.
Stop 4 · Bagyo

PAGASA weather office

  • White two-storey government office, blue band, a radar dome on the roof.
  • The lattice mast from today moves here, with its wind cups and red typhoon flags.
  • A typhoon signal board by the door that steps 1 → 5 when you park there, the same scale Bagyo scrolls through.
  • Moves: cups spin, flags flutter, the radar dish rotates.
  • North row (tall mast, far side). ≈ 7 m, mast 13 m.
Stop 5 · Recipe Finder

Carinderia

  • Single-storey eatery under a big scalloped tarpaulin awning on poles.
  • A counter of steel pots (ulam), a menu board, blue plastic tables and stools out on the sidewalk.
  • Moves: steam curls up from the pots (a small particle sprite).
  • East block, facing the plaza. ≈ 5 m.
Last stop · About Ian

Jeepney terminal at the pier

  • Where every route starts and ends: a long shed roof, hanging route boards, queue rails.
  • Two or three parked jeepneys waiting for passengers; the pier and the bangka carry on behind it into the bay.
  • Moves: a jeepney pulls out and loops the town now and then; the bangka rocks.
  • North centre, on the bay. Low (≈ 5 m) with the open bay behind.

3. The rest of the town

  • Shophouses built from one generator: 1–3 storeys, a few colours, with or without balconies, signs, aircon units, water tanks. Seeded, so the town is the same every visit.
  • Street life: electric poles with sagging wires (cheap lines), lampposts, a waiting shed, banderitas across one street, plants in pots by doors.
  • Traffic: 3–4 tricycles and 1–2 other jeepneys on fixed loops. They slow behind you instead of crashing; you bump into them like props.
  • Plaza: fountain (water shader), shade trees that sway, benches, a basketball court in the south-east block.
  • Bay: seawall promenade with palms and a railing, the pier and bangka, the sea shader with glints and cloud shadows.

4. Keeping the jeepney in view

  • Buildings stay low (≤ 3 storeys) except the two landmarks on the far side, as the island already does.
  • Any building between the camera and the jeepney fades out with a soft dither (one uniform on the shared material, no extra draw calls), the way most city-builder games handle it.
  • The camera stays where it is now (12 up, 20 back) and may tilt a little steeper if the streets feel tight. That's a Leva slider to try before deciding.

5. Driving

  • Colliders gain rectangles (blocks, buildings) alongside today's circles (props, poles, parked vehicles).
  • The autopilot follows the street grid: it picks the shortest way through the intersections, then steers along it, instead of curving round obstacles. Tap-to-drive snaps your tap to the nearest street.
  • Top speed may rise a little (the town is bigger than the island), tuned with the existing sliders.
  • driving.check.ts is rewritten for the grid: every stop reachable from 200 random spots, 500 random taps arrive, nothing gets stuck on a corner.

9. Questions for you

  • People: none (recommended for now), or a few simple figures at the plaza and terminal?
  • Stop order: keep the current numbering, or renumber along the route as you'd drive it?
